Abstract Integrated Sensory and Communication (ISAC) is a key supporting technology for emerging 6G applications, with its core lying in the design of dual-function waveforms. Although Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) is a commonly used basic waveform, its original version has inherent uncorrelated auto-correlation characteristics, which are deficient in sensory performance; moreover, its sensitivity to Doppler shift also limits its widespread application in dynamic scenarios. To address these issues, this paper proposes an Overlap-Indexed Modulation OFDM (S-IM-OFDM) scheme. This scheme enhances sensory performance without additional power consumption by transferring the energy efficiency of Indexed Modulation OFDM (IM-OFDM) to the perception-oriented signals of OFDM, while utilizing the parameters sensed to compensate for Doppler shift in time-varying channels, maintaining excellent communication performance. Introduction Integrated Sensory and Communication (ISAC) has attracted widespread attention from both academic and industrial communities [1]. In various studies on ISAC, researchers have been dedicated to developing dual-function waveforms to achieve simultaneous execution of wireless communication and radar perception [2^].
In the multitude of waveform designs, Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) is often chosen as a candidate for dual functionality implementation due to its minimal need for significant modifications to the air interface [3]-[6]. However, it should be noted that OFDM has two fundamental flaws in supporting both perception and communication functionalities: in the perception aspect, due to the randomness of transmitted information, sidelobes are unpredictable, making perception capabilities difficult to predict; in the communication aspect, Carrier Interference Coding (CIC) in time-varying channels leads to performance degradation. To enhance the precision of OFDM perception, literature [7] employs a high-resolution estimation algorithm - 2D-MUSIC; to optimize the theoretical performance of target detection, literature [8] focuses on the power allocation optimization of OFDM subcarriers. Nonetheless, these methods cannot fundamentally resolve the inherent high sidelobe problem of OFDM. In terms of communication performance optimization, literature [9] proposes an auto-elimination scheme through adjacent subcarrier cancellation of ICI, but at the expense of spectral efficiency; literature [10] introduces a beam-domain Doppler compensator that requires large-scale antenna assistance for beam-domain projection.
This paper proposes an enhanced OFDM scheme, called S-IM-OFDM, to address the shortcomings of existing solutions. S-IM-OFDM combines transparent sequences with IM-OFDM waveforms: IM-OFDM, through meticulous design, can transmit information bits at lower power consumption [11]-[15], and has shown potential in ISAC applications [16]-[17]; the overlay of transparent sequences enhances perceptual capabilities by improving autocorrelation characteristics. Both components of S-IM-OFDM are applicable for target perception, and their joint estimation results can improve the accuracy of distance-velocity estimates. Moreover, the inherent perceptual capability of S-IM-OFDM can actively compensate for Doppler effects, significantly reducing the impact of ICI in time-varying channels. Unlike the method in [16] that inserts radar signals into some idle subcarriers, this paper utilizes all subcarriers of IM-OFDM to ensure no information loss and achieve a broader perceptual spectrum; compared to the index modulation scheme in [17] that only embeds information in radar signals, this method simultaneously utilizes subcarrier indices and symbol transmission bits, thereby enhancing the reachable spectral efficiency.
In addition to elucidating the construction and working principle of S-IM-OFDM, this paper also optimizes its dual functionality performance through theoretical analysis. Simulation results confirm the advantages of S-IM-OFDM over existing OFDM related schemes, highlighting its significant potential to solve the traditional OFDM defects and making it an ideal candidate solution for ISAC applications, especially suitable for challenging time-varying channel scenarios.
Conclusion This paper proposes a novel dual-function waveform S-IM-OFDM for ISAC applications. This waveform effectively addresses the inherent limitations of OFDM by integrating communication-oriented index modulation energy efficiency with perceptive-oriented transparent sequence superior autocorrelation characteristics. The perception and communication performance can be flexibly adjusted through the power allocation ratio. Extensive simulation results have verified the dual-function advantages of S-IM-OFDM: in terms of perception, its distance-velocity estimation accuracy surpasses that of OFDM; in terms of communication, S-IM-OFDM consistently maintains a significant coding gain compared to OFDM, especially under time-varying channels. Future work will focus on extending this technology to millimeter-wave massive MIMO systems.
